# Microsoft 365 Copilot — Prompting Starter Guide
### For FinBridge Office Staff

---

## Welcome

Microsoft 365 Copilot is built into the tools you already use — Outlook, Word, and Excel. It can save you real time on everyday tasks: drafting emails, summarising long documents, cleaning up spreadsheets, and more.

This guide gives you a simple formula for writing good prompts, then shows you exactly what to type in each app. No technical knowledge needed.

---

## The "Good Prompt" Formula

A good Copilot prompt answers four quick questions. You don't need all four every time, but the more you include, the better the result.

```
TASK + CONTEXT + FORMAT + TONE
```

| Part | What it means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Task** | What do you want Copilot to do? | *"Write an email"* |
| **Context** | What is it about? Who is involved? | *"to a supplier asking for a revised invoice"* |
| **Format** | How should the output look? | *"in three short paragraphs"* |
| **Tone** | What style should it use? | *"professional but friendly"* |

**Easy to remember:** Think of it like giving instructions to a very capable new colleague — the more context you give them, the less back-and-forth you need.

## Copilot in Outlook

Copilot can draft replies, summarise long email threads, and adjust tone before you hit send.

> **How to open it:** Inside an email or reply, look for the Copilot button (a small sparkle icon ✨) in the toolbar. Click it and a prompt box will appear. Copilot can already see the email or thread you have open, so when the examples below say "this thread" or "my reply", that is exactly what it means — you do not need to copy and paste anything.

### Example 1 — Draft a chaser email
> **Prompt:**
> *"Draft an email to our accountant chasing the Q3 budget report. It was due last Friday. Keep it polite but clear that I need it today. Two short paragraphs."*

### Example 2 — Summarise a long thread
> **Prompt:**
> *"Summarise this email thread in five bullet points. Focus on any decisions made and any actions I need to take."*

### Example 3 — Soften a reply
> **Prompt:**
> *"Rewrite my reply below so it sounds less blunt and more collaborative. Keep the same meaning."*

## Copilot in Word

Copilot can draft documents, rewrite sections, and adjust the length or tone of existing text.

> **How to open it:** Click the Copilot button (✨) in the Home ribbon, or look for it in the right-hand panel. It can see the document you have open.

### Example 1 — Create a first draft
> **Prompt:**
> *"Write a one-page briefing note for the finance team explaining the new expenses policy. Cover: what has changed, what staff need to do differently, and who to contact with questions. Use plain English."*

### Example 2 — Shorten existing text
> **How to use it:** Highlight the paragraph you want to shorten, then open Copilot and type:
> *"Rewrite this paragraph so it is no longer than four sentences and easier to read for someone not familiar with finance."*

### Example 3 — Add a professional summary
> **Prompt:**
> *"Write a short executive summary for this report. It should be no more than 100 words and highlight the three most important findings."*

## Copilot in Excel

Copilot can explain formulas, write new ones for you in plain English, and help you spot patterns in your data.

### Example 1 — Explain a formula
> **How to use it:** Click on any cell that contains a formula you do not understand. Copy the formula, then open Copilot and type:
> *"Explain what this formula does in plain English: [paste your formula here]"*
>
> For example: *"Explain what this formula does in plain English: =AVERAGE(B2:B20)"*

### Example 2 — Write a formula for you
> **Prompt:**
> *"I have a column called 'Status' in column D and a column called 'Amount' in column E. Write a formula that adds up all the amounts where the status is 'Approved'."*

### Example 3 — Spot patterns in data
> **Prompt:**
> *"Look at the data in this table and tell me which three months had the highest total spend, and whether there is any obvious trend."*

## Using Copilot Safely — Nothing to Worry About

The good news: Microsoft 365 Copilot runs inside FinBridge's own systems. It is not a public chatbot like the ones you might have seen in the news, and your prompts are not shared with the internet.

That said, there is one simple habit worth building, and it only takes a second:

> **Ask yourself: would I be comfortable saying this out loud in a meeting room full of colleagues?**
> If yes, you are fine. If not, describe what you need without including the real details.

In practice, this mainly means avoiding things like real client names, account numbers, or anything that says "Confidential" or "Private" at the top. You rarely need to paste that kind of information anyway — Copilot is most useful when you describe the task, not when you dump raw data into it.

**Easy workaround:** Instead of pasting a client's actual details, describe the situation. For example:
- Instead of: *"Here is Jane Smith's account — write a letter about her missed payment"*
- Try: *"Write a polite letter to a customer about a missed payment, asking them to get in touch"*

You get an equally useful result, and there is no risk involved.

This is the same habit introduced on Day 1 of this programme: when in doubt, swap real details for a general description. It takes seconds and quickly becomes second nature.
