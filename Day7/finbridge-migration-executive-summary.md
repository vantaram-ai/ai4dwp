The overall picture is positive. Most staff appear to have settled into the new Windows 11 setup, and many of the early migration problems reported in the first week have now eased. Sign-in problems, account lockouts, missing files, slow logins, and unstable remote access all show signs of improvement, and a growing share of feedback describes the experience as smooth and reliable. In practical terms, this suggests the migration has stabilised for most users, with the main disruption now concentrated in a smaller number of specific issues rather than broad, widespread failure.

What remains open is more targeted but still important. The most visible unresolved issue is the printer on floor 3, which has continued long enough to affect confidence and is now causing avoidable workarounds for staff. A separate new concern has emerged around Excel becoming unstable when larger spreadsheets are opened, which is particularly disruptive for teams working with heavier files. Alongside this, device performance data points to a slowdown in startup times for one business group shortly after a recent settings change, and that is being reviewed as a priority. The current approach is to keep users informed, contain the remaining disruption with simple workarounds where possible, and focus technical effort on the few issues that are still affecting day-to-day productivity.
